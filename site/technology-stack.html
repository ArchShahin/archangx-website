Technology Stack

The space data centre, layer by layer

Seraphim is the software and protocol stack that turns a constellation of satellites into a data centre in orbit. It spans 21 layers, from the orbit itself to the applications people use, and seven planes that cut across all of them: security, sovereignty, regulation, risk, the digital twin, operations and engineering governance. Every layer is designed together, and every layer is modelled in our digital twin before anything flies.

Band 1 · L01–L02

Physical and orbit

Where the data centre lives, and what the environment does to it.

L01

Site, civil and orbit environment

What it does. Defines where the data centre sits and everything the environment throws at it: orbit geometry, sunlight and eclipse, radiation, debris and space weather. On the ground, the same layer covers the sovereign ground core sites and gateway stations in Canada.

Technologies and standards

Low Earth orbit constellation design (Walker-star); J2 orbital perturbation models; space-weather feeds (Kp index, solar particle events, polar-cap plasma vortices); conjunction data messages for debris; ECSS and NASA environment standards.

Seraphim design choices

  • 4 orbital planes × 12 satellites at 550 km, in multi-LTAN sun-synchronous planes; 12 per plane is the minimum that keeps every satellite in line of sight of its neighbours.
  • The dawn–dusk plane is the power reference; eclipse seasons of the other planes (up to ~20 min) are budgeted explicitly.
  • Space weather is an operating input, not a footnote: a polar-cap vortex detector catches "space hurricanes" that Kp-based rules miss.
  • Ground core and first gateways in Canada, starting with Saskatchewan, so the system of record never leaves the country.

Key numbers

48 satellites · 550 km · eclipse ≤ ~20 min · design dose ~150 rad/year behind 10 mm aluminium.

Watched by. Space-weather agent (M13), debris and space-traffic agent (M14) · twin: orbit and illumination model.

L02

Structures, racks and mechanical

What it does. Holds the data centre together through launch and for years in vacuum: compute modules, radiator panels, deployable arrays and the mechanical paths that carry heat.

Technologies and standards

Launch-qualified structures; random-vibration and shock qualification; thermal-vacuum (TVAC) testing; conduction-cooled module mounting; ECSS-E-ST-32 structural standards.

Seraphim design choices

  • Conduction-only design: there is no air, so every watt must travel through metal to a radiator.
  • Compute is packaged as replaceable tiles so nodes can be refreshed on a roughly four-year cycle.
  • Heavy parts (capacitor cans, toroidal magnetics) are bolted, not glued, to survive launch.

Key numbers

~4-year compute refresh cycle · design life to end-of-life deorbit.

Watched by. Launch and installation agent (M15), hardware-ageing agent (M16) · twin: structural/thermal coupling.

Band 2 · L03–L05

Power and thermal

Energy is the scarcest resource in orbit; heat is the hardest to get rid of.

L03

Power conversion and distribution

What it does. Converts and routes electricity from the arrays and batteries to every load, at the highest possible efficiency, with protection against faults.

Technologies and standards

High-voltage DC bus (300–800 V); silicon-carbide (SiC) and gallium-nitride (GaN) converters; solid-state power controllers; distributed-gap ferrite magnetics; film DC-link capacitors; lessons from terrestrial 800 V DC and solid-state transformers.

Seraphim design choices

  • High voltage means lower current, lighter harness and lower losses; partial discharge and arcing in near-vacuum are designed against explicitly.
  • Rad-hard GaN (qualified to ~300 V today) is the switching technology of choice; commercial SiC is derated because heavy ions can burn it out well below its rating.
  • Solid-state breakers and SSPCs for routine protection; mechanical contactors only as one-time safing switches.

Key numbers

1 point of conversion efficiency ≈ 4 kg per 10 kW node · 95 % → 98.5 % saves ≈ 750 kg per MW of radiator and array.

Watched by. Power and energy agent (M1) · twin: power-chain and bus-balance model.

L04

Energy generation and storage

What it does. Makes energy from sunlight and stores enough to run through eclipse, while protecting the battery so the satellite lasts.

Technologies and standards

Solar arrays; Li-ion cells (space-qualified, or upscreened automotive cells at constellation scale); battery management systems; on the ground, second-life EV battery storage for the sovereign core.

Seraphim design choices

  • Battery state of charge never goes below a 30 % protection floor, whatever the workload.
  • Compute jobs are classed by importance (Class I mission-critical, II important, III deferrable), so energy shortfalls shed the least important work first.
  • Forecasts of sunlight and load feed the scheduler a full orbit ahead.

Key numbers

Reference node: 1.2 m² array, 451 W, 500–600 Wh battery · simulated noon-plane minimum state of charge 77.9 % · ~5,500 eclipse cycles per year.

Watched by. Power and energy agent (M1), fleet-health agent (M11) · twin: generation and battery model.

L05

Thermal and cooling

What it does. Moves heat from chips to radiators and sheds it into space, and absorbs the short, intense bursts that AI workloads produce.

Technologies and standards

Radiators; heat pipes and pumped loops; phase-change material (PCM) thermal buffers; cold plates; thermal-network (RC) modelling.

Seraphim design choices

  • Radiators are sized to the orbit-average load; PCM absorbs bursts and refreezes in the idle half-orbit, so the radiator doesn't have to be sized to the peak.
  • The PCM's melt fraction is a term in the scheduler's decision, so heavy tasking can't silently exhaust the buffer.
  • A receding-horizon optimiser (linear programme, ~10 ms per step) plans compute against power and heat together.

Key numbers

Radiator 1.8–3.3 m² per kW · PCM 0.4–0.8 kg · optimiser cuts peak PCM melt from 80 % to 35 % with the same work done.

Watched by. Thermal agent (M2) · twin: RC thermal network with PCM enthalpy.

Band 3 · L06–L08

Compute, storage and network hardware

The silicon.

L06

Compute hardware

What it does. Runs the workloads: AI inference and training, data processing, and the deterministic processing of sensor frames.

Technologies and standards

Radiation-tolerant GPU and TPU-class accelerators; NVIDIA edge-class modules (Jetson/IGX-class) for development and hardware-in-the-loop testing; FPGAs; radiation-hardened microcontrollers (e.g. Microchip HPSC class).

Seraphim design choices

  • Two kinds of compute: FPGAs for deterministic work (frame hashing, signing, the physics gate), accelerators for AI.
  • Radiation effects are measured and designed for: silent data corruption is caught by a physics verifier rather than assumed away.
  • Sensor frames are hashed and signed in the FPGA, before any software can touch them.

Key numbers

400 W × 300 s compute bursts · one silent data corruption per ~14–20 rad on TPU-class hardware ≈ 9 per chip-year · only ~1 % of GPU silent errors show up as NaN/Inf.

Watched by. Compute and radiation agent (M3) · twin: workload and accelerator model.

L07

Storage hardware

What it does. Keeps data safe on board: raw frames briefly on sensing satellites, products and history on aggregation nodes.

Technologies and standards

Radiation-tolerant NAND flash with strong error correction; scrubbing; write-once evidence partitions.

Seraphim design choices

  • Raw frames live in a ring buffer and are dropped once their event is safely copied to a neighbour.
  • Evidence (signed events, audit logs) is write-once, so nothing can be quietly altered.

Key numbers

Sensing payload ~4.3 Mb/s · per-node backlog ≤ 17 kB with four ground stations and in-plane relay.

Watched by. Data platform agent (M5) · twin: storage and backlog model.

L08

Network hardware and fabric

What it does. The physical network: optical terminals between satellites, radio and optical terminals to the ground, and the onboard links from sensors to compute.

Technologies and standards

Optical inter-satellite terminals (1550 nm); Ka-band and optical ground terminals; SpaceWire and SpaceFibre; onboard packet switching.

Seraphim design choices

  • Two optical terminals per aggregation node, one forward and one aft in the plane, forming a ring.
  • A wide-beam RF terminal as fallback when optical alignment drops at polar crossings.

Key numbers

10 Gb/s per optical terminal · 20 Gb/s per plane to an optical ground station.

Watched by. Network and contact agent (M4) · twin: ISL and link-state model.

Band 4 · L09–L13

Links, protocols and platform

How the machine talks to itself and to the ground, and how software runs on it.

L09

Links, radio, optical and spectrum

What it does. Every link the system uses, its availability, and the spectrum it needs.

Technologies and standards

CCSDS 141.0-B-1 optical communications; Ka-band with DVB-S2X adaptive coding and modulation; CCSDS AOS/USLP framing; 3GPP non-terrestrial network (NTN) links (Releases 17–19, toward 6G in Release 21); ISED and ITU spectrum filings.

Seraphim design choices

  • Eight link classes, each with a defined role: in-plane optical (backbone), cross-plane optical (when available), Ka feeder, optical downlink, RF fallback, 6G NTN, sensor I/O, telemetry and command.
  • A predictive seven-state link machine driven from the contact plan: make-before-break, not break-then-recover, with hysteresis on every transition.
  • The alert path never depends on the cross-plane mesh.

Key numbers

In-plane optical available 100 % of the orbit · cross-plane 77 % (0 % below 10° latitude) · Ka feeder 1.5 Gb/s per station · 6G NTN Doppler ±50.6 kHz at 2 GHz.

Watched by. Network and contact agent (M4) · twin: link geometry and availability model.

L10

Protocols, routing and transport

What it does. Moves data reliably across a network where links come and go on a schedule, and keeps it secure hop by hop.

Technologies and standards

Delay-tolerant networking: Bundle Protocol v7 (RFC 9171) with BPSec (RFC 9172); contact-graph routing; custody transfer; CCSDS TM/TC with Space Data Link Security (SDLS).

Seraphim design choices

  • Custody: a satellite that captures data hands a copy to its in-plane neighbour within 10 seconds, so losing one satellite can't lose data.
  • Routing from the predicted contact plan, not from discovery, because the geometry is known in advance.
  • Alerts travel on a priority lane with a short per-hop authentication code; the full post-quantum signature follows on the durable path.

Key numbers

Time to safe ground storage (p95, four stations): 81.8 min store-and-forward → 1.5 min with in-plane relay → < 0.2 min with a full mesh · daily data exposure 2.4 % → 0.1 % with custody.

Watched by. Network and contact agent (M4), security agent (M7) · twin: contact simulation and max-flow egress.

L11

Firmware, OS and virtualization

What it does. The trusted base everything else runs on: how a node boots, updates and isolates workloads of different importance.

Technologies and standards

Secure boot with hardware root of trust; A/B firmware images with anti-rollback; hardened Linux; lightweight virtualization and partitioning for mixed-criticality workloads; remote attestation (IETF RATS).

Seraphim design choices

  • Every node proves what it is running before it is trusted (attestation no older than 24 hours).
  • Updates are atomic and reversible; a rollback can never be used to install an older, vulnerable version.
  • Mission-critical functions (detection, alerting) are isolated from deferrable workloads.

Key numbers

Attestation age ≤ 24 h · two firmware slots per node.

Watched by. Security agent (M7), operations and change agent (M17) · twin: FDIR and fault-injection model.

L12

Platform services

What it does. How software is packaged, deployed, scheduled and updated, in orbit and on the ground.

Technologies and standards

In orbit: lightweight Kubernetes (K3s/KubeEdge class) and WebAssembly runtimes, designed for clusters that lose contact. On the ground: Kubernetes (Google Kubernetes Engine Autopilot with confidential nodes), Terraform, CI/CD and GitOps.

Seraphim design choices

  • "After Kubernetes": the in-orbit scheduler must keep working for an hour without a control plane, so decisions are made locally and reconciled later.
  • Placement is energy-, heat- and link-aware: a job runs where there is power, thermal headroom and a path to its consumer.
  • Every deployed artefact (container, model, policy, prompt) is signed and content-addressed.

Key numbers

Misses on a 20-minute delivery budget: 18–29 % with no relay → 0.5–2.3 % with learned placement.

Watched by. Operations and change agent (M17) · twin: workload placement model.

L13

Observability, SRE and IT operations

What it does. Sees everything, in every layer, and turns it into decisions a small team can act on.

Technologies and standards

OpenTelemetry (metrics, logs, traces); SLO burn-rate alerting; Grafana / Cloud Monitoring; anomaly detectors (interquartile-range thresholds on forecast residuals, CUSUM, Poisson fleet statistics).

Seraphim design choices

  • One telemetry pipeline for protocols, services and agents alike.
  • Health is a margin vector per layer, with the binding limit named, not a single score; stale data is reported as UNKNOWN, never as fine.
  • Three layers of outlier detection: inside the computation (physics verifier), per telemetry channel, and across the fleet.

Key numbers

Physics verifier catches 49 % of silent errors by count and 97 % by consequence at 2 % extra compute · telemetry fusion recall 0.91 · fleet detector finds a doubled error rate in 15 steps with no false alarms.

Watched by. Fleet-health agent (M11) and every macro-agent · twin: produces identical telemetry for rehearsal.

Band 5 · L14–L16

Data

From raw pixels to a governed, replicated, sovereign record.

L14

Data ingestion and streaming (Zone 0)

What it does. The event backbone every other component reads from and writes to.

Technologies and standards

Kafka-style append-only log (Apache Kafka / Strimzi on the ground; Pulsar-style tiered storage considered in orbit); MQTT and CoAP at the sensor edge; a bridge over Bundle Protocol v7.

Seraphim design choices

  • Ten topics carry everything: raw optical, thermal and SAR frames; health telemetry; wildfire and perimeter events; compute jobs; tasking plans; priority alerts; model updates.
  • At-least-once delivery with content-hash identity, so duplicates are harmless.
  • Custody before publish: a record is visible only once its neighbour holds a copy.
  • A hash-chained log: every event carries the hash of the one before, so nothing can be removed unnoticed.

Key numbers

10 topics · custody within 10 s · alert packet 149 bytes.

Watched by. Data platform agent (M5) · twin: Zone-0 event log (the agents run on twin events unmodified).

L15

Data storage, lakehouse and caching (Zones 3–4)

What it does. Keeps data where it is needed, as fresh as it needs to be, and makes the sovereign ground core the system of record.

Technologies and standards

Lakehouse tables (Apache Iceberg), Parquet and Zarr; object storage with Object Lock for evidence; on the ground, BigQuery, Bigtable and Spanner-class stores.

Seraphim design choices

  • Four tiers: edge ring buffer → plane hot cache → plane lakehouse → sovereign ground core.
  • Caches are sized by each product's staleness budget (its update cadence plus one missed delivery) and pre-fetched from the contact plan.
  • Replication is write-through within a plane and eventually consistent across planes.

Key numbers

Recovery after a 2-hour partition: 196 MB, no data loss · update reaching all 48 satellites (p95): 88 min store-and-forward → 3.7 min with in-plane relay.

Watched by. Data platform agent (M5) · twin: storage, cache and replication model.

L16

Data governance, catalogue and quality

What it does. Knows what every piece of data is, where it came from, who may use it and where it may go.

Technologies and standards

STAC catalogue and ISO 19115/19157 metadata; conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs) for the catalogue; data lineage; Dataplex-class governance on the ground.

Seraphim design choices

  • Four sovereignty classes: S1 public products, S2 imagery, S3 defence-relevant (in-region only), S4 restricted (air-gapped, never in orbit).
  • The catalogue converges even after a plane has been out of contact for an orbit.
  • Lineage is ordered by producer sequence numbers, not GNSS time, so a spoofed clock can't reorder history.

Key numbers

Catalogue 106 GB/year if fully replicated → 290 MB plus 1.75 GB of stubs with hash references.

Watched by. Data governance agent (M10), sovereignty checks in the security agent (M7) · twin: catalogue convergence model.

Band 6 · L17–L21

AI, agents and applications

Where the data centre starts to think.

L17

AI/ML models and perception (Zones 1–2)

What it does. Turns sensor data into decisions on the satellite, and optimises the constellation as a whole.

Technologies and standards

Thermal-infrared, mid-wave infrared and visible imagery; physics-based fire detection (Planck-law thresholds); small convolutional neural networks; optimisation (linear programming, MILP, adaptive large-neighbourhood search); learned contact prediction.

Seraphim design choices

  • A physics gate first (cheap, explainable, removes 93 % of false candidates in clean scenes), then a small neural verifier on the survivors.
  • A seven-state fire tracker with dual thresholds and persistence, so one hot frame never becomes a false alarm to a fire crew.
  • Classical solvers for tasking: our own analysis shows they solve in milliseconds for constellations under 50 satellites. Quantum stays a benchmarked research path, not a dependency.
  • No large language model runs on a sensing satellite.

Key numbers

Detection ≥ 99.6 % for a 20 m² fire · onboard detection 41–56 ms · alert budget ~16 events/day.

Watched by. AI/ML agent (M6), wildfire mission agent (M8) · twin: synthetic fire scenes and detector evaluation.

L18

MLOps and model lifecycle (Zone 5)

What it does. Trains, versions, ships and monitors models across a constellation that is rarely fully connected.

Technologies and standards

Federated learning over inter-satellite links; low-rank adapters (LoRA); model registry and signing; drift monitoring; Vertex AI-class training on the ground.

Seraphim design choices

  • Models learn where the data is: small adapters (~80 MB) are trained in orbit and aggregated over the ISL mesh.
  • Every model and every detection threshold is a signed, versioned artefact.
  • A human approves every promotion to production.

Key numbers

Federated round (ViT-B): 0.28 s over a 10 Gb/s ISL vs 28 s through a 100 Mb/s ground contact.

Watched by. AI/ML agent (M6) · twin: model-update propagation.

L19

Agentic AI and assurance plane

What it does. Lets a small team run hundreds of satellites: agents watch every layer, explain what they see and recommend actions.

Technologies and standards

Macro-agents, MicroAgents and Skills; Model Context Protocol (MCP) with read-only tool allow-lists; pinned models; conformal prediction for faithfulness; OpenTelemetry span tracing.

Seraphim design choices

  • A routing-only orchestrator calls macro-agents, one per layer; each runs a fixed workflow over MicroAgents (a prompt, an allow-list of tools and a pinned model), which use deterministic Skills.
  • Agents observe and recommend; they never write to the command bus, the alert topic or model updates.
  • Every numeric claim must match the cited events exactly; every qualitative claim passes a statistical faithfulness gate or goes to a human.
  • An independent guardian, with its own model and no tools, blocks anything that could reduce safety, disclose restricted data or displace human authority ("Rule Zero").

Key numbers

20 macro-agents · 84 MicroAgents · 32 Skills · prototype found all 8 injected faults with zero writes to action topics · ~3 human reviews per day.

Watched by. Agent-governance agent (M9) and the guardian · twin: agents run on twin events unmodified.

L20

APIs, microservices and integration

What it does. How customers, partners and other networks connect to Seraphim.

Technologies and standards

Event APIs on the Zone-0 topics; REST and gRPC services; Apigee-class API management; agent-to-agent interfaces; 3GPP interfaces for on-board service hosting and computing-context transfer between satellites.

Seraphim design choices

  • Every external call is authorised on identity, attestation, capability, freshness, data class, budget and policy version.
  • Partner constellations and 6G networks connect through standard interfaces rather than custom integrations.

Key numbers

Hosting 40–60 % of services on board cuts feeder traffic from 1,100 to 660–450 Gb/s · context transfer between satellites ≤ 100 MB.

Watched by. Tenant-operations agent (M12), security agent (M7) · twin: workload-source model.

L21

Applications and user experience

What it does. What people actually use.

Technologies and standards

Alert delivery to agencies and responders; operator consoles; dashboards; a bilingual public website; the digital twin offered as a service.

Seraphim design choices

  • Wildfire detection and alerting for CIFFC, provincial agencies and responders.
  • Early warning for defence and NORAD.
  • Compute and data services for satellite operators and Canadian carriers (Telus, Bell, Rogers, Québecor Média).
  • The digital twin as a product: operators test their constellation before they launch it.

Key numbers

Alert to ground ~1.5 min (p95) vs ~3 hours for today's satellite fire products.

Watched by. Wildfire mission agent (M8), tenant-operations agent (M12) · twin: end-to-end scenarios.

Seven planes that cut across every layer

What holds the stack together

XSEC

Security and zero trust

A verified identity for every node and every agent; attestation no older than 24 hours; post-quantum signatures (ML-DSA, FN-DSA) and key exchange (ML-KEM); BPSec on every bundle; policy enforced at every hop; 26 threat classes modelled, from GNSS spoofing to attacks on the security system itself.

XSOV

Sovereignty and data governance

Data classes S1–S4 decide where data may travel and be processed; the most sensitive never leave an air-gapped Canadian enclave; a residency guard blocks any route that would break a commitment, even during a storm.

XREG

Regulation and compliance

42 obligations, each traced to a test and a monitor: space licensing (RSSSA), spectrum, privacy (PIPEDA, Québec Law 25, GDPR), the EU AI Act and Data Act, export controls, and the standards our cryptography and telemetry follow.

XRSK

Risk and resilience

71 registered risks with key risk indicators, from launch to end of life; a network of risk agents for space weather, debris, hardware ageing and regulation; Monte Carlo loss modelling for common-cause failures.

XDT

Digital twin and simulation

A physics-based twin of the whole constellation that couples orbit, power, heat, links, data and workloads; 50 of 50 automated tests passing; every layer above has a model in it.

XOPS

Mission and service operations

Operations designed so a small team can run hundreds of satellites, with humans approving every consequential action.

XPGM

Programme, QA and engineering governance

Requirements traced from layer to test; seven test levels with gates; AI-assisted delivery with human-owned golden records.
