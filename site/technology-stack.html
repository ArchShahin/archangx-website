Technology Stack

The space data centre, layer by layer

Seraphim is the software and protocol stack that turns a constellation of satellites into a data centre in orbit. It spans 21 layers, from the orbit itself to the applications people use, and seven planes that cut across all of them: security, sovereignty, regulation, risk, the digital twin, operations and engineering governance. Every layer is designed together, and every layer is modelled in our digital twin before anything flies.

Band 1 · L01–L02

Physical and orbit

Where the data centre lives.

L01

Site, civil and orbit environment

Low Earth orbit at 550 km; 4 planes × 12 satellites in a Walker-star constellation with multi-LTAN sun-synchronous planes; the dawn–dusk plane is the power reference. Radiation, atomic oxygen, debris and space weather (storms, solar particle events, polar-cap "space hurricanes") are design inputs, not afterthoughts.

L02

Structures, racks and mechanical

Launch-qualified compute modules, radiator panels, deployables, vibration and thermal-vacuum qualification; conduction-only mounting because there is no air to cool anything.

Band 2 · L03–L05

Power and thermal

Energy is the scarcest resource in orbit; heat is the hardest to get rid of.

L03

Power conversion and distribution

300–800 V DC bus; silicon-carbide and gallium-nitride converters; solid-state power controllers. Every point of conversion efficiency saves about 4 kg per 10 kW node; going from 95 % to 98.5 % saves roughly 750 kg per megawatt.

L04

Energy generation and storage

Solar arrays (reference node 1.2 m², 451 W), Li-ion batteries (500–600 Wh) with a 30 % protection floor; eclipse seasons budgeted plane by plane.

L05

Thermal and cooling

Radiators sized at 1.8–3.3 m² per kW; phase-change material buffers (0.4–0.8 kg) absorb compute bursts and refreeze in the idle half-orbit; a receding-horizon optimiser keeps battery and heat within limits.

Band 3 · L06–L08

Compute, storage and network hardware

The silicon.

L06

Compute hardware

Radiation-tolerant GPU and TPU-class accelerators (400 W bursts); FPGAs for deterministic sensor processing and frame signing; radiation effects measured, not assumed (about one silent data corruption per 14–20 rad on TPU-class hardware).

L07

Storage hardware

Radiation-tolerant flash with ECC; ring buffers on sensing satellites; object storage on aggregation nodes.

L08

Network hardware and fabric

Optical inter-satellite terminals (two per aggregation node), Ka-band and optical ground terminals, onboard switching; SpaceWire / SpaceFibre from sensors to compute.

Band 4 · L09–L13

Links, protocols and platform

How the machine talks to itself and to the ground, and how software runs on it.

L09

Links, radio, optical and spectrum

In-plane optical ISLs at 10 Gb/s (CCSDS 141.0-B-1), available 100 % of the orbit; cross-plane optical ISLs available ~77 %; Ka-band feeders at 1.5 Gb/s per station with DVB-S2X adaptive coding; optical downlinks; wide-beam RF fallback; 3GPP non-terrestrial network (NTN) links toward 6G.

L10

Protocols, routing and transport

Delay-tolerant networking with Bundle Protocol v7 and BPSec; contact-graph routing from the predicted orbit; custody transfer to an in-plane neighbour within 10 seconds, so no single satellite loss can lose data; CCSDS TM/TC with SDLS for command and telemetry.

L11

Firmware, OS and virtualization

Secure boot, A/B firmware updates with anti-rollback, hardened Linux, lightweight isolation for mixed-criticality workloads.

L12

Platform services

Containers and orchestration designed for clusters that disconnect: lightweight Kubernetes (K3s/KubeEdge-class) and WebAssembly runtimes in orbit; Kubernetes, Terraform, CI/CD and GitOps on the ground; energy- and heat-aware workload placement.

L13

Observability, SRE and IT operations

One OpenTelemetry pipeline across every layer; a margin vector per layer instead of a single health score; SLO burn-rate alerting; three layers of outlier detection (inside the computation, per telemetry channel, across the fleet).

Band 5 · L14–L16

Data

From raw pixels to a governed, replicated, sovereign record.

L14

Data ingestion and streaming (Zone 0)

A Kafka-style event backbone bridged over the delay-tolerant network: ten topics from raw thermal frames to signed alerts and model updates; MQTT/CoAP at the sensor edge; content-hash identity and a hash-chained log so every event can be audited.

L15

Data storage, lakehouse and caching (Zones 3–4)

Lakehouse tables (Apache Iceberg / Parquet / Zarr) on aggregation nodes; three caching tiers sized by how fresh each product must be; replication to the in-plane neighbour; the sovereign ground core in Canada as the system of record.

L16

Data governance, catalogue and quality

STAC metadata and a conflict-free replicated catalogue that converges even after a plane has been out of contact; lineage, quality checks and four data-sovereignty classes (S1 public to S4 restricted, air-gapped).

Band 6 · L17–L21

AI, agents and applications

Where the data centre starts to think.

L17

AI/ML models and perception (Zones 1–2)

Onboard wildfire detection: a physics gate (thermal-band rules) plus a small neural verifier, ≥ 99.6 % detection for a 20 m² fire; constellation-level optimisation for tasking, placement and energy. No large language model runs on a sensing satellite.

L18

MLOps and model lifecycle (Zone 5)

Small adapters (LoRA) trained by federated learning across satellites; versioned, signed models; drift monitoring; a human-approved promotion gate.

L19

Agentic AI and assurance plane

Macro-agents, one per layer (power, thermal, compute, network, data, AI, security, mission, governance, risk), run MicroAgents that use deterministic Skills over read-only views of the event log. Agents observe and recommend; they never command. A statistical faithfulness gate checks every claim, and an independent guardian blocks anything that could reduce safety.

L20

APIs, microservices and integration

Event and REST APIs for operators and customers; standard interfaces for partner constellations and 6G networks (on-board service hosting, computing-context transfer).

L21

Applications and user experience

Wildfire detection and alerting for agencies and responders; early warning for defence; compute and data services for satellite operators and Canadian carriers; the digital twin as a product.

Seven planes that cut across every layer

What holds the stack together

XSEC

Security and zero trust

A verified identity for every node and every agent; attestation no older than 24 hours; post-quantum signatures (ML-DSA, FN-DSA) and key exchange (ML-KEM); BPSec on every bundle; policy enforced at every hop; 26 threat classes modelled, from GNSS spoofing to attacks on the security system itself.

XSOV

Sovereignty and data governance

Data classes S1–S4 decide where data may travel and be processed; the most sensitive never leave an air-gapped Canadian enclave; a residency guard blocks any route that would break a commitment, even during a storm.

XREG

Regulation and compliance

42 obligations, each traced to a test and a monitor: space licensing (RSSSA), spectrum, privacy (PIPEDA, Québec Law 25, GDPR), the EU AI Act and Data Act, export controls, and the standards our cryptography and telemetry follow.

XRSK

Risk and resilience

71 registered risks with key risk indicators, from launch to end of life; a network of risk agents for space weather, debris, hardware ageing and regulation; Monte Carlo loss modelling for common-cause failures.

XDT

Digital twin and simulation

A physics-based twin of the whole constellation that couples orbit, power, heat, links, data and workloads; 50 of 50 automated tests passing; every layer above has a model in it.

XOPS

Mission and service operations

Operations designed so a small team can run hundreds of satellites, with humans approving every consequential action.

XPGM

Programme, QA and engineering governance

Requirements traced from layer to test; seven test levels with gates; AI-assisted delivery with human-owned golden records.
