Eight panels

Hosted and chaired by ArchangX

Each panel has an ArchangX chair who sets the questions. Invited speakers from industry, government and research are named once they confirm.

Panel 1 · 09:50–10:35

Qubits in Orbit

Quantum Computing for Space Data Centres

Where quantum helps and where it doesn't. Our own peer review taught us that for constellations under about 50 satellites, classical solvers (MILP/ALNS) solve re-tasking in milliseconds, so the honest question is when quantum annealing or gate-model machines start to win, and how a quantum job queue fits into an orbital data platform. Also: post-quantum cryptography (ML-DSA, FN-DSA, ML-KEM) on hardware whose root keys can never be replaced, and quantum key distribution over optical inter-satellite links.

Panel 2 · 10:55–11:40

From Space Weather to Sovereignty

Risk and Resilience

The risk register for an orbital data centre: geomagnetic storms and “space hurricanes”, radiation and silent data corruption, debris and conjunctions, launch and installation, and regulatory risk such as data residency during a storm reroute. Monte Carlo loss, common-cause failure, insurance and who pays.

Panel 3 · 11:45–12:30

Data Platforms in Orbit

From Kafka to the Lakehouse

The elements of a data platform that has to work when the network doesn't: event streaming (Kafka, Pulsar, MQTT at the edge), the delay-tolerant bridge (BPv7 with custody), lakehouse tables (Apache Iceberg, Parquet, Zarr), catalogues (STAC, CRDT), caching tiers, governance and lineage. Which market leaders' designs survive orbit, and which assumptions break.

Panel 4 · 13:30–14:15

Power, Heat and Silicon

Infrastructure for Compute in Orbit

The hardware layers: radiation-tolerant GPUs and TPUs, high-voltage DC buses and solid-state conversion (SiC, GaN), phase-change thermal buffers and radiators, optical terminals. Every point of conversion efficiency is kilograms of radiator. What terrestrial AI data centres are learning (800 V DC, liquid cooling, solid-state transformers) that space can borrow.

Panel 5 · 14:20–15:05

Macro-Agents and Skills

Agentic AI That Runs Infrastructure

How a few people can run hundreds of satellites: macro-agents per layer, MicroAgents with pinned models and MCP allow-lists, deterministic Skills, a statistical faithfulness gate and an independent guardian. Why agents should observe and recommend, never command, and how zero trust applies to agents themselves.

Panel 6 · 15:25–16:10

After Kubernetes

Orchestrating Clusters That Disconnect

Kubernetes assumes a control plane you can always reach. Orbit doesn't offer one. WebAssembly runtimes, unikernels, K3s and KubeEdge, DTN-aware schedulers, energy- and heat-aware placement, and GitOps when your cluster is out of contact for an hour.

Panel 7 · 16:15–17:00

AI Sovereignty, 6G and Self-Organizing Networks

Who controls the data, the models and the network when compute moves to orbit. Sovereign ground cores, residency classes, 3GPP Release 21 non-terrestrial networks, on-board service hosting, and self-organizing networks that heal themselves around a lost link. Includes Seraphim's Canada-first connectivity plan: ground core and first feeder connections to Canadian data centres, starting in Saskatchewan (central, low-latency to the boreal fire belt, low-cost power and land), with dedicated service tiers for Canadian carriers and media companies.

Panel 8 · 17:05–17:50

Fire, From Lightning to War

Detection for Canada and the World

The application that proves the stack: onboard wildfire detection, early warning in the North, and a global fire index with a goal of zero uncontrolled fire. Closing theme: fire that is seen cannot be hidden; observability over fire as a contribution to peace.
